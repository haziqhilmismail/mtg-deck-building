# You're Building Your Commander Decks Backwards

Source: Based Deck Department, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7lJJPXGfFNs (transcript only)

## Start with the end

Most weak decks have no clear picture of the winning board state or how the deck reliably gets there. How the deck wins should affect every card in it, including the early and mid game. This is why some decks want lots of draw or ramp and others none.

## Questions to answer before touching the list

1. What bracket is this deck for, and what experience should it create?
2. How does the deck win, and which combinations of cards matter most for that win?

## Bracket sets the clock and the mana budget

| Bracket | Game ends around | Total mana across the game |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | turn 9 to 10 | about 45 |
| 3 | turn 7 | about 35 |
| 4 | turn 4 | about 15 |

Assumes an early ramp spell and every land drop. Do not rely on longer games or extra ramp.

- Bracket 2: casual; do not be oppressive; should not win before turn 9.
- Bracket 3: shutting the game down around turns 5 to 7 is acceptable.
- Bracket 4: must win reliably by about turn 4 or 5. This is why combos dominate there.
- The same card changes value by bracket: Static Orb is miserable in bracket 2 and mild in bracket 4; Grave Pact is too slow for bracket 4 and fits bracket 3.

## The mana budget check

1. List every card that must be cast to reach the winning state (commander, engine, enablers, finisher, plus needed ramp, draw and removal).
2. Add their mana values.
3. Compare with the bracket's total mana.

If the win costs too much, do one of three things: add ramp, change the win condition to fit, or move the deck to a lower bracket. If the win is far cheaper than the bracket's budget (a two-card, six-mana combo in bracket 3), the deck is overpowered for that bracket.

- A win built from cards the deck wants to play anyway can use more of the budget (example: about 25 mana in bracket 3). A control deck whose win conditions do not synergize needs a win made of fewer, cheaper cards.
- Count turn cycles as well as mana when the win grows over time (example: a 10 to 13 mana win that still needs about seven turn cycles fits bracket 2).

## Other rules

- Cut toolbox cards that are too slow for the bracket and are dead in the opening hand, even if fun.
- A commander can be a bad fit for a bracket. If the commander's cost is a large share of the game's total mana (example: about 30% in bracket 4) and it needs many turns in play, lower the bracket instead of forcing it.
- Forcing a slow, flavourful commander into a high bracket fills the deck with generic power cards and loses the identity the owner wanted.
