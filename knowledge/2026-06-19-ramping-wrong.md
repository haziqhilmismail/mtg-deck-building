# You're Probably Ramping Wrong

Source: Based Deck Department, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=upqHyQqN3WU (transcript only)

Conflicts with: `2025-04-21-template-by-the-numbers.md` (11 ramp to have one by turn 2) and `2025-04-04-30-years-10-lessons.md` (10+ ramp). This file says 12, aimed at turn 3.

## Numbers

- **Total mana sources: 45 to 50** in the average deck (the video also says 40 to 50 once), lands plus ramp, in any bracket. Low-land decks reach this with dorks or free mana.
- **Ramp is binary: 12 or 0.** If the deck wants ramp it wants it by about turn 3 every game, and 12 sources does that. With 36 to 38 lands this lands in the right band.
- **Six mana rocks means the game plan is unclear.** Go back and define the plan first.
- **About 20 ramp is too many** in most decks: hands fill with ramp and lands and nothing to cast.
- Ramp costs at least 10% of the deck, or 15% of the nonland cards.

## Questions, in order

1. What is the deck ramping towards, by which turn? Does it need ramp at all?
2. What is the game plan: which spells on which turns, and what is the ideal curve?
3. What kind of deck is it, and which ramp cards fit that?

Ramp is only worth it if it lets specific key cards be played earlier than land drops alone would. That gives hard numbers to aim for.

## Rules

- **No autopilot ramp.** Example: Cultivate in a deck with a 3-mana commander and nothing to ramp towards.
- **Two kinds of greed.** Cutting lands and ramp for more payoffs, and "speed greed": spending many slots to cast the commander one turn earlier, leaving nothing to follow it with.
- **Turn-3 ramp has the highest opportunity cost.** Turns 1 and 2 are usually empty anyway; turn 3 could be a real play.
- **Do not skip a slot on the curve.** Ramping from 2 straight past 4 removes the 4-mana cards from the deck.
- **Prefer fewer, more explosive ramp pieces when the payoff is big spells after the commander** (example: Bloom Tender, Fanatic of Rhonas, Selvala over Birds, Rampant Growth, Kodama's Reach, for a 5-mana commander on turn 4 and 6 to 7 mana on turn 5). Slots saved go to 4-mana plays and haste.
- **Match the ramp type to the deck:**
  - Aggro or combo aiming at one big turn: one-shot bursts (rituals, treasures). A treasure is as good as a land there.
  - Decks casting a big spell every turn: repeatable sources that stay, not one-shot bursts.
  - Decks that care which card type is in the library or graveyard: choose rocks or dorks to suit (rocks when creature count must stay pure; dorks when you want them to die).
  - Commander is the ramp: little or none needed.
  - Low-curve aggro playing threats on curve: zero ramp on purpose. Every ramp slot is a slot not spent on pressure.
- **Everything is a trade.** Adding one thing removes another: fewer lands risks stalling, more draw spends the turn the ramp was meant to buy.
