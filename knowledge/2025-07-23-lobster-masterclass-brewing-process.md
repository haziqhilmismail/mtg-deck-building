# How I Turned a Lobster Into a Win Machine (Deck Building Masterclass)

Source: Based Deck Department, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJ5Jq05HbuI (transcript only). Worked example: Ragost, Deft Gastronaut. The 33-card method itself is in `2025-04-27-33-card-deck-method.md`.

## Brewing process, in order

1. **Mind map** what the commander wants. Look at EDHREC only if you lack direction; following it blindly gives as much bad as good.
2. **Character sheet:** list the groups of cards the deck needs. A deck fits about five groups. More than that and it turns unfocused and inconsistent. Reason: about 12 copies of something are needed to draw it reliably every game, and 12 x 5 = 60, roughly the nonland count.
3. **Describe the perfect turn cycle** and cost it in cards and mana. That shows what the deck needs next (mana, a draw engine, protection, removal).
4. **Draft a 33-card deck** (one third of the list, same groups in proportion) before searching widely. It makes the brew easy to see and quick to goldfish.
5. **Search Scryfall with broad terms**, build a long list (about 200 here), then carve down.
6. **Goldfish the 33 and analyse it.** Ask what is working and what is not, and change the plan; do not only check that the deck "does something".
7. **Fill out to 100** by tripling the 33 from the shortlist. Small category deviations are fine: if 95% of the deck is focused, that is enough.
8. **Test the 100 and fix what testing shows.**

## Rules that came out of the example

- **Cost the win in total mana.** Here the first plan needed 30 or more mana out of about 60 available by turn 10, over half, so it was dropped.
- **If one card solves the deck's core problem, build around finding it:** add tutors, accept the deck plays more like combo, then add backup engines for redundancy. A deck that does nothing without its engine needs redundancy, not more payoffs.
- **Prioritise card draw over extra fodder or tokens** until the engine is found; drawing smooths the deck far more.
- **Pick the single best version of an effect and skip the direct downgrades,** even ones popular on EDHREC (here: lifelink over Soul Warden-style lifegain).
- **Leave out damage amplifiers and extra payoffs that only work once the engine is running.** Make the primary win reliable first. Brewing is more about what is left out than what goes in.
- **Protection preference:** cheap instant hexproof or protection spells, because targeted removal is the main threat; a board wipe hurts the whole table.
- **Board wipes should spare your commander.** The example ran 3 such wipes and 6 targeted removal.
- **Upgrading a bracket:** swap in tutors and game changers that find the engine, and say which card each one replaces.

## Piloting notes that affect building

- Mulligan priority: engine or tutor, then the enabler, then protection, with at least three mana sources.
- Do not rush the commander out without protection in hand.
- Manage threat: start slow so opponents do not identify you until life totals are in the mid-20s.
