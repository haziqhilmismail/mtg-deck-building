# Upgrading EDH Decks As A Chronic Optimizer

Source: The Trinket Mage, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZRt4cCR6Lp4 (transcript only)

Conflicts with: `2025-07-23-lobster-masterclass-brewing-process.md` ("Upgrading a bracket: swap in tutors and game changers that find the engine"). This file says tutors add consistency, not power; they only raise the bracket when the play they find is already too strong for it.

## Two different jobs

- **Upgrading** raises the deck's power and can move it up a bracket. **Optimizing (tuning)** makes the deck do its thing more reliably without changing how strong that thing is. Decide which one the owner wants before touching a card.
- A change that does not affect power ceiling, speed, or resilience is tuning, not an upgrade.

## The three things that raise power

1. **Power ceiling:** the strongest play pattern the deck can ever produce, judged on its ideal hand. One fast, hard-to-interact-with combo raises the ceiling even if it shows up rarely (example: Thassa's Oracle + Demonic Consultation can win on turn 3, which is not appropriate for bracket 2 or 3 however inconsistent it is).
2. **Speed:** winning on an earlier turn makes more of the table's interaction useless, and the effect grows as the turn gets lower (win on turn 7 and an 8-mana wipe is too slow; win on turn 4 and a large share of interaction is useless). Speed only measures decks with a definitive win condition (combo, overrun, damage doubler, large X spell).
3. **Resilience:** more card draw, recursion, board wipes, or anything that scales into the late game. Use this as the yardstick for decks with no definitive win button, such as value and control decks. Swapping a weak draw spell for a much better one (example: The One Ring) is a power upgrade.

## What is only tuning

- **Ratios.** Fix the mix of enablers and payoffs for this commander. If the commander is the enabler, the 99 wants more payoffs; if the commander is the payoff, the 99 wants more enablers. Card draw and other enablers in the list change the right number.
- **Swapping a synergy piece for a better copy.** Replacing the third or fourth best version of an effect with a new second best does not move a bracket, because the deck's strongest play has not changed.
- **Consistency.** Consistency does not equal power. A consistent deck with a weak plan stays weak; removing tutors from a deck with a too-strong plan does not lower its bracket, because the strongest play is unchanged.

## Keeping a deck in a low bracket while still tuning it

- **Add a hoop:** an extra step the plan must clear (example: needing A plus B in play for a long time). Hoops add points of interaction and slow the deck, so it can be tuned hard without becoming oppressive.
- **Pick an inherently weaker theme** (example: Boros control). It stays fair however well it is tuned.
- **Use a build restriction:** a very low budget (example: $10 or less), one block or set, or bulk-bin cards only.
- A tuned low-bracket deck should feel like a well-oiled machine: powerful without feeling unfair.

## To move a deck up a bracket on purpose

- Work on power ceiling, speed, and resilience, in that order of clarity. Other changes will not make it feel significantly stronger.
